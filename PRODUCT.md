# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Freight forwarder internal users and their customers use this portal during everyday logistics operations. Internal tenant admins, sales, operation, and finance users manage rates, quotes, bookings, SO, basic shipments, documents, invoices, users, and customer records. Customer admins and customer users search freight rates, review quotes, create and maintain bookings, view SO details, follow shipment progress, and read billing information for their own customer company.

## Product Purpose

Freight Customer Portal V1.1 helps small and mid-sized freight forwarders run a customer-facing ocean FCL workflow from Rate to Quote to Booking to SO to Basic Shipment with clear tenant and customer-company boundaries. Success means a first-time customer or operator can complete the core chain without training, without understanding internal database states, and without asking a developer what each action means.

## Brand Personality

Clean, professional, operational. The product should feel calm, trustworthy, and work-focused for logistics teams handling time-sensitive freight information. It should avoid decorative flourish and instead make the next business action obvious.

## Anti-references

Do not make the product feel like a consumer app, marketing website, dashboard template, dense ERP panel, or concept mockup. Avoid gratuitous cards, heavy shadows, decorative gradients, decorative animation, meaningless icons, and a redesigned brand system. Do not expose internal operation language to customer users when a simpler customer-facing status is enough.

## Design Principles

1. Task-oriented before database-oriented: each page should answer where the user is, what object they are viewing, what status it is in, what matters now, and what happens after the next action.
2. One clear primary action per business stage: secondary actions stay visually quieter, and destructive actions require explicit context.
3. Business language over technical language: statuses, errors, confirmations, success feedback, and buttons describe real logistics outcomes.
4. Reuse and standardize: shared formatters, status metadata, empty states, loading states, and action patterns should carry the experience across Rate, Quote, Booking, SO, and Shipment.
5. Preserve V1.1 scope: improve clarity, predictability, and recoverability without adding new business objects or changing the frozen status semantics.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use baseline accessible product UI practices: semantic buttons and links, associated form labels, visible focus states, status text that is not color-only, inline field errors, keyboard-friendly dialogs, and responsive layouts that remain usable at common desktop widths. Chinese UI copy and logistics abbreviations must remain readable without relying on English enum values.
